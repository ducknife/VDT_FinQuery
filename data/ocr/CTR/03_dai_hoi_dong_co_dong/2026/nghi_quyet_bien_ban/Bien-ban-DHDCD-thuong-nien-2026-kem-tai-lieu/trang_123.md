- Recovery of consumer demand: After a period of tightened spending following the pandemic, household consumption demand is expected to rebound strongly from 2026.

- However, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) forecasts that Vietnam’s growth may decline to 5.6% from 6.5% in 2026 due to anticipated challenges in the global economic environment. In addition, major trading partners such as the United States and China are also expected to face difficulties. The World Bank (WB) and the Asian Development Bank (ADB) estimates Vietnam’s growth at 6.1% and 6.4% respectively in 2026.

  - Although the economy is expected to maintain stable growth, 2026 is still forecast to present many challenges for businesses. Labor shortages—particularly of high-quality technical personnel—are occurring across multiple industries. As of the end of 2024, the proportion of the workforce with formal training and certifications was only 28.3%. In the construction sector, some companies have introduced “competitive” wage policies to attract talent. In addition, natural disasters and storms are expected to remain unpredictable. Meteorological agencies forecast that 2026 may see fewer storms than average, as the global climate system transitions from La Niña to a neutral phase and potentially toward El Niño. However, this also implies more complicated and less predictable weather patterns, posing potential risks to various economic sectors.
